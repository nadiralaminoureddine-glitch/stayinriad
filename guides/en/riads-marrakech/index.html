Riads in Marrakech

Staying in a riad means living Marrakech from the inside: behind a discreet door in the medina, a courtyard with orange trees, a fountain and a rooftop terrace, a few minutes' walk from Jemaa el-Fna square.

Which area should you choose?

MouassineNorth of Jemaa el-Fna, close to the souks and the Mouassine fountain. Quiet lanes, art galleries and Le Jardin Secret: ideal for a first visit.
Riad Zitoun and the KasbahTo the south, near Bahia Palace, El Badi Palace and the Saadian Tombs. Lively and easy to reach by taxi.
The MellahThe old Jewish quarter, next to Bahia Palace. More local, riads are often more affordable.
Bab Doukkala and Sidi MimounMore residential, close to the medina gates: handy if you arrive by car.
LaksourBetween Jemaa el-Fna and Mouassine: right in the centre, everything within walking distance.

When to go

Spring (March to May) and autumn (October–November) are the most pleasant. Summer is very hot, often above 40 °C: pick a riad with a pool or plunge pool. Winter days are mild but nights are cool: check that the room is heated.

Our tips

  • Ask the riad to meet you at the edge of the medina: taxis cannot drive into the lanes.
  • A rooftop terrace is worth it for breakfast and sunset.
  • Tourist tax is paid on site, on top of the room price.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best area to stay in a riad in Marrakech?

For a first visit, Mouassine and Laksour: you are in the heart of the medina, near the souks and Jemaa el-Fna, in quieter lanes. Riad Zitoun and the Kasbah are handy for visiting the palaces.

Can I reach my riad by car?

Rarely to the door: most riads are on pedestrian lanes. The riad usually sends someone to meet you at the car park or the nearest medina gate.

When is the best time to visit Marrakech?

Spring and autumn. Summer is very hot; winter is mild by day but cool at night.
