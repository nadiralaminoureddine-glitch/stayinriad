Riads in Fez

Fes el-Bali is one of the largest medinas in the world and a UNESCO World Heritage site. Its riads, often former merchant family homes, hide some of the finest zellige tiles, carved plaster and cedar wood in Morocco.

Which area should you choose?

Bab BoujloudAt the entrance of the medina, near the Blue Gate. Easy taxi access and the start of the two main streets, Talaa Kebira and Talaa Seghira.
Talaa KebiraThe main street running down to the Qarawiyyin: in the heart of the souks, madrasas and craftsmen.
BathaNear the Batha Museum and Bab Boujloud: quiet and well connected, ideal for easy arrivals and departures.
Andalusian quarterAcross the river, quieter and less touristy, near the Andalusian Mosque.
Fes JdidThe 'new' Fez, near the Royal Palace and the Mellah: more open, a good base.

When to go

Spring and autumn are perfect. Summer is hot and winter cold, especially at night: in winter, check that the riad is heated.

Our tips

  • The Fez medina is a real maze: let the riad guide you on your first day.
  • Pick a riad near a gate (Bab Boujloud, Batha) if you have big suitcases.
  • Go up to a rooftop at sunset: the view over the medina is unique.

Frequently asked questions

Which area should I choose for a riad in Fez?

Bab Boujloud and Batha for easy access; Talaa Kebira to be in the heart of the souks; the Andalusian quarter for more peace and quiet.

Can you get lost in the Fez medina?

Yes, it happens a lot: there are countless lanes. Most riads offer to pick you up and can recommend an official guide.

When should I visit Fez?

Spring and autumn. Winter nights are cold and summer is hot.
